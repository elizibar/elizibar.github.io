## The Year of Final Fantasy

We decided, half-jokingly, to do the ["Year of Final Fantasy"](/yearoffinalfantasy/) as a project, and even back then it was pretty obvious that it was going to take more than a year to play through. A typical JRPG experience takes a lot of time, and the more I looked into it the more Final Fantasy games, movies, novels, and so on that I discovered. It got hard to catalog it all.

For those who aren't in the know, the Year of Final Fantasy is the attempt to play every Final Fantasy game that I can get my hands on. Mostly this means playing remastered versions, but I am leaving the door open to playin on actual hardware for the ones I already own. We're using this site to track all that, capture how the games made me feel, and also write up what kind of information that's interesting that was can, like the fact that there are novelizations of some of the early Final Fantasy games that were never translated to English. (Who knew?)

While we might be in the middle of **Final Fantasy VIII Remastered**, we have so far crunched through 18 different Final Fantasy games, though a couple of them we didn't play through all the way. (I'm looking at the mobile games here.) Having to rush Ever Crisis because of the End of Life of the game was a bit unexpected, since it was originally set to be one of the later Final Fantasy VII experiences.

Prior to starting "The Year" we had already played FF7 Remake, The Zodiac Age version of FF12, and even rocked Final Fantasy XVI which was a very different, and very hand-cramping experience.

As a community we've taken in some of the Final Fantasy media, from the old FF5 anime to Spirits Within and the FF7-related animated features. And I've been taking on reading through the books as I get them, including some unofficial related media like the tome about the Greek myths that inspired FF7.

Over on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/elizibar) we're still playing out the Final Fantasy XVI videos we recorded a while back, but soon we'll start the Year of Final Fantasy videos in earnest over there, so if you missed out on the live stream the VODs are coming, I promise!

So join us on stream for soem Final Fantasy VIII fun.

Also we've got reviews / verdicts for a lot of these things written up, though I'm going to go revise a lot of the material because it's disjointed right now. I've got links down below in the link dump.

### Final Fantasy VIII

So far this replay of Final Fantasy VIII, via the Remastered version on Steam, has been great. The graphics are updated just enough to take some of the PSX clunk off of them, Triple Triad is still a hoot of a card game, and some of the music is catchy enough that I hum it in my head while I'm doing things like going for a walk, or driving.

I'll try to write up some more thoughts about the way Magic works in VIII for the next edition of this newsletter.

On the negative side, in the last stream or two I did get frustrated at some of the game's systems.

The way Triple Triad rules spread (or don't) and all the pointless RNG associated with it just pissed me right off while I was trying to spread the ALL rule to Balamb. Thankfully the [Hyne Save Editor](https://www.nexusmods.com/finalfantasy8/mods/5?tab=description) exists, and that rectified the heck out of that problem.

Being able to miss things, so easily, is also frustrating. I'm not talking about something minor like missing a battle at Fort Condor in Final Fantasy VII, an event which having went to great pains to fight all of them, seems like a "who cares" at the end of the VII playthrough. No, in VIII you can straight up miss core pieces of the game, like the Battle Meter that shows party stats. Sure it's not essential, but why do you have to go out of your way to get it - in fact I'm pretty sure I did go out of my way to get it and still didn't. This was the original reason I went looking for the Save Editor, resolving my Triple Triad annoyances and saving me hours of work and reloads was just a great side benefit.

And then there was the whole Chocobo Forest sidequest / puzzle. I'm not sure who thought this was going to be a lot of fun, because I found it more of a chore than anything else. The rules seemed inconsistent from place to place, sometimes you'd be playing Lights Out with the chocobos, sometimes you'd have to get one of them to bowl the others off the map, with no indication of what you were supposed to do. Plus traveling to the last location for the quest was quite an ordeal.

### Greek Myths that Inspired Final Fantasy VII

So far I'm in Chapter 5 of this book. I haven't as much time for reading as I might like, which also explains the dearth of comic book reviews on the YouTube channel, so I'm not as far in as I'd have liked to be. Having said that, so far, I'm around page 90, and the book hasn't hit its stride yet. We're still introducing the greek cosmology and making comparisons with Final Fantasy VII that either are obvious (Gaia / Gaea) or feel like a bit of a stretch.

I'm hopeful that it's going to pick up as we get deeper into this book and start tackling individual stories instead of laying the groundwork for the deeper discussions.

This also makes me think about Final Fantasy VIII and the influences underneath it because that game world feels unique even among Final Fantasy games with their sometimes outlandish settings.

## FEARtober 2026

Just about every year for almost 10 years now, we've done [FEARtober](https://elizibar.github.io/feartober/) on [Twitch](https://www.twitch.tv/elizibar), with the VODs going up on YouTube during October, and wrapping up come the end of November or maybe the end of the year. We try to play a F.E.A.R. game, which in more recent years has led to some creative exploration of the modding scene for F.E.A.R.

### EchoPatch

Because of the Year of Final Fantasy we didn't get going into **FEARtober** until the last week of October, but we made pretty good use of the time. We started off with [F.E.A.R.](/feartober/fear/) of course, and like last year we played the OG F.E.A.R. with a mod, the really awesome [EchoPatch](https://github.com/Wemino/EchoPatch). Last year we rocked the Prototype Mod and it did a great job of rejuvenating the game, but the EchoPatch was next level, modernizing the game and making it a much smoother experience.

The other day [John Markley](https://www.twitch.tv/johnmarkley) went to play F.E.A.R. and clearly hadn't visited the Steam forums before hand on how to get it working, because that old game needs a little bit of love and massage before it will work. So while I was hanging out in his Stream chat, I got to tell him about the EchoPatch and it worked like a charm for him. It feels real good to share something and see it immediately improve someone's life, even if just for a little while. Mad props to the developer of EchoPatch.

### Halloween Scary Stream

And then things lined up so nicely. The night of Halloween was also the night for doing the Fall Back, so I put on a stream meant to last through the BONUS HOUR of the year. I megadosed on riboflavin (this is a reference) and got to relive the same hour all over again, playing indie horror games. We started by experiencing the updated content for **No, I'm Not a Human**, **the static speaks my name**, **Emily is Away**, **A Date in the Park**, **The Black Whale**, and **Medusa's Labyrinth**.

No, I'm Not a Human was great to revisit and it was pretty amazing just how quickly I started to remember things about the game. While the new characters and ending were neat, the RNG made things frustrating to try and get to see all the characters, get the new achievements, and so on. But man, that game has some really great aesthetic.

The other games were all ones that were free on Steam, so you can go check them out any time. The experiences vary quite a bit, but they're either interesting if flawed (A Date in the Park), just scary enough for Halloween fun (Medusa's Labyrinth), or just sort of brutally depressing (The Black Whale). It was a great stream, and I'm glad so many people were able to hang out with me for so much of it. It made my Halloween night extra special.

## Movies

### Resident Evil (2026)

**Short plot summary**: Delivery guy talks to girlfriend while driving, runs over someone, then proceeds to run in terror for 90 minutes being the biggest idiot I've seen on film in a while. Like a soap opera with monsters.

This is probably a controversial take, but I kind of liked it. The first third is Grade A horror. It's a shame that the film veered off into jokey camp in the second act, and I generally dislike endings that are clever and "dark" when the film hasn't actually earned respect for it. If the film had been named something other than Resident Evil I think it would have generally been better received. I for sure appreciated that the protagonist didn't immediately know how to use a gun, because there is no way that guy had any idea what he was doing.

Other things that felt right: The implacable monster stalking the protagonist like Nemesis or Mr. X and the fact that like every RE game I've ever played it started out strong and got dumb by the ending.

### Transformers One

**Short plot summary**: Two goofy robots go on a journey of self-discovery and start a civil war.

Really solid **Transformers** origin film. I liked the relationship between Megatron and Optimus, it makes the rest of the Transformers lore feel a lot more like the inevitable Shakesperean tragedy that it is.

I didn't like how Optimus was constantly shown up by other characters, and I didn't like how the tone veered into post-modern joking every time it was getting serious. I wish I'd watched the 80's movie right after, now in hindsight.

### The Killer Shrews

**Short plot summary**: Hungry dogs eat stuff.

I watch a lot of bad movies, and boy **The Killer Shrews** is a bad one. It's a "classic" horror film about science gone bad, with poor acting, no budget, and bad writing. This was maybe the third or fourth time I've seen it, and this time it was the Nightmare Theater presentation on PBS.

Not much to say but the "shrews" have to be seen to be believed. It's a shame they kill the one character I like in the whole film off so early on.

Though by bad movie standards it's not **that** bad, it's no Red Zone Cuba at least, and it does have a few fun moments in it.

### Abbot and Costello Meet Frankenstein

**Short plot summary**: See the title.

Watched the **Svengoolie** presentation of this film, AGAIN. It's like the 10th time I've watched it via Sven, and I don't know, the 100th time overall in my life. I love this movie, it's just one big laugh interspersed with just enough seriousness to let Lon Chaney's wolfman and Lugosi's Dracula (only the 2nd time he ever was actually Dracula) shine. Having watched the Invisible Man Returns for the first time recently, I finally get the Vincent Price voice cameo at the end.

10/10, will watch it again and again and...

## Anime

### Nadia: The Secret of Blue Water

I watched a couple random episodes of this show a month or so back, but I'm seriously watching it now. It's been fantastic. It's fun, it's well paced, it's bright, and everything is so very earnest. In some regards, it feels like a proto-Evangelion. You can tell the same people who worked on that show previously worked on this. I feel sad that I didn't watch this many years ago when I was younger, because it's a special treat.

There's an online FAST linear TV channel that my DVR gets and I've been watching it off that, and it's pretty cool.

### Little Nemo: Adventures in Slumberland

**Short plot summary**: Kid dreams really hard, learns hard life lessons, and fight nightmares.

This was a fun animated feature, combining Japanese and Disney styles in both its animation and storytelling. It's a wonderful fusion of styles and a visual treat. There are one or two places where it doesn't pop, but they don't last, and the movie is soon back to showing off lots of fun things and rolling along on a fun adventure. An absolutely amazing lineup of people worked on it over the years, and the development of the film is as interesting a read as the movie was to watch.

The Gertie the Dinosaur cameo was also a treat, really showcasing the roots of the film in Windsor McKay's work a century ago.

Maybe a 7/10. But I'm amazed this movie lost so much money and wasn't actually a hit.

## Cool Stuff

### RetroTap Power Distribution System

As I start to reorganize parts of my home office, and my living room, I've been looking at some of the interesting technology out there to help consolidate things down so there are fewer wires being run, less of a disorganized mess, and so on.

On X (aka Twitter), I saw someone talking about the [RetroTap](https://kytor.com/store/product/retrotap/). It looks neat, and while I'm not sure I'll go for it, primarily because of the price, it's something I'm considering as I try to figure out how to redo my stream setup and more easily enable myself to stream from my consoles.

### Unmanaged Network Switches

Previously, I've always repurposed old routers as switches for providing internet access to my videogame consoles. But a month or so ago I bought a couple of dirt cheap unmanaged switches and I've been absolutely happy with theem. They're fast, they require no setup or maintenance or ROM flashes. They just work.  Sure these are just gigabit switches, but that's all I really neeed, and for 20 bucks or less these things are meeting my need at a great price point.

### Powerline Networking

This is another thing I picked up about a month ago, and I'm also really surprised at how well this worked. I needed to deliver a network connection to part of my house that's far from where my router is, and after looking at things like wireless mesh devices, this seemed like an even more ideal solution. It lets me get a fast network connection with minimal fuss, no finicky wifi packet loss, no fighting the neighbors over frequency. It's maybe a bit pricey, but over the last month it's been rock solid.
