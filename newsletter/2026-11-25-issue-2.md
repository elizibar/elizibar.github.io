## The Year of Final Fantasy

### Final Fantasy VIII 

This past week we got throught he big battle, the big plot reveal, the next big plot reveal, and made it to where we can see one of Final Fantasy 8's big tragedies, a tragedy that the characters never really learn just how tragic it is. I don't think it's too much of a spoiler, after 27 years or so, to mention that we get to go into outer space. Technically this is the third time the series has taken a trip into the void between worlds, but really in FFIV you're just doing so to get to the Moon, space itself is just a road for the Lunar Whale to travel. In FFVII and FFVIII, Outer Space is itself the destination because there are things to do in it. The science in FFVIII is a little more tight, because the amount of propellant and air involved feels more scarce and in space those are the two things that are going to determine how long you get to live. What a great sequence - and with the Lunar Cry in the background, it feels even crazier.

### Squall is only kind of Dead

I haven't had a chance to see if anyone else has come up with this yet, and I bet someone has, but I had a realization, and it ties in thematically with things from both Final Fantasy VII and Final Fantasy VIII.

I'm pretty sure when Edea kills Squall he becomes a Guardian Force. He just doesn't know it, and neither does anyone else.

It sounds crazy right? But in Final Fantasy VII we had someone who refuses to stay dead (Sephiroth), that rebuilds his body, and returns to life to battle the heroes.

Final Fantasy X has a character who's dead, but still hanging around, and a character that isn't even real.

I was reading Gallagher's book, still pretty early on, about Poseidon and Oceanus and how water relates to the Cetra and it just sort of clicked in my head.

I'm going to try to follow-up on this in succeeding commentary, because this feels more right than the mere "Squall is dead" theory which is trip, unsatisfying, and negates the whole point of the game's narrative. This instead fits right in: Squall's desire to not be forgotten, the power of the sorceress, and the exchanging of a totem to summon Squall (his ring).

I bet someone's already thought of all this, but it was new to me!

### GTAVI

The elephant in the room. Everyone is either playing it or making a show out of not playing it.

Me? I never finished GTAV. It looks pretty, but it sounds like they fundamentally started to take the sociopathy out of the series. One of the great joys in any GTA game was causing complete havoc, doing terrible things to random NPCs who didn't do anything to deserve what's happening, and edgy jokes. I see people talking about how you can't shoot up place X or person Y and it feels very un-GTA to me.

But then maybe that's because I never played GTA5 and the precedent was established in those games.

I'm also appalled by the budget and number of people that worked on this game: a billion dollars and over 6000 people.

That's incredible.

Imagine all the other games that could have been financed for that kind of money, all the new creative ideas that could have been tried out.

I hope that the people playing it have a good time, and I'm sure one day I'll give it a go if it ever goes on sale (it won't), it just doesn't seem like as big a deal to me as the GTA 3 trilogy did. Vice City, despite its age, still has peak aesthetic.

It is pretty wild though that the game was nominated for a pile of rewards... before it ever came out. Remember how there used to be this thing called E3 and a thing called CES and people got to tell developers how awful their stuff was? But now it's all reward shows and hoi polloi aren't allowed to be involved so that nothing interrupted the backslapping and gladhanding of a bunch of out of touch people. "Don't you people have phones" indeed.

Also I see that the hacking community is already getting started on GTAVI with [leeks of executable code](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/grand-theft-auto/cyberleek-releases-executable-for-gta-6-xbox-build-leaked-last-week-the-torch-passes-to-the-open-source-recomp-and-emulation-projects/) for decompilation and porting work. ### Outages

See because the thing is, it doesn't matter why the user is having a bad experience. You can explain it, find all the excuses you want, but at the end of the day if the user has spent a lot of money expecting a certain experience and your product didn't provide it, it's no good.

That goes for my personal experience, but also millions of others. I have a friend who hasn't been able to get into her laptop for days because the Apple login doesn't work. Millions of people doing critical work, like at hospitals or other emergency services, were unable to communicate via email.

Once upon a time, the internet was distributed into dozens of small services, each serving their own group of users, and those services were allowed to interoperate and communicate with each other. This is how the internet was architected. It was never meant to have a few monolithic service providers. In the old days, an outage would inconvenience a subset of people and the internet at large would route around the damage. These days, everything is tried to centralized logins and services, and being banned or disrupted means that entire regions of life become inaccessible for essentially everyone.

The death of the user-centric, decentralized internet, and even the less-centralized early internet 2.0 set the world up to experience massive systemic failures. These outages would have happened one way or another, the adoption of AI for security, coding, and hacking purposes merely sped this up.

A lot of things live on the internet that really have no right being there.

The internet was designed for the open exchange of scientific information, and to a lesser extent a way for nerds to send each other stupid jokes. (Or today, memes.)

It was never designed for your bank to send financial information, all your family photos, medical records, or any other sensitive or private information. Attempts to graft security onto it fail for several reasons, not the least of which is that the internet has to continue supporting old, insecure protocols and methods long past their prime in order to maintain compatibility with hardware and software stacks that only slowly evolve due to budgetary and process constraints.

It's a shame that instead of building new, more secure networks, with new infrastructure and new protocols, we built core functionality on top of something that was never meant to be this important or central to life.

I don't know what we could have done differently. Each step made a lot of sense, kind of like PC manufacturers who kept outsourcing parts of their production to companies like Acer, Asus, or Lenovo that slowly learned how to build the entire computer and then turned around and ate the original suppliers. Each decision improved the bottom line, right up until the moment there was no longer a bottom line to improve. The Stupendium has a line in a song that goes like "It's easy to cut out the middle-man, once he's cut out most of himself."

The modern internet is a lot the same way. Each decision to put more onto it made sense, each step forward made a lot of sense, each consolidation felt like a good choice, leading through the rise of major social media platforms and eventually to the point today where most of the internet is 'protected' by CloudFlare. It all made sense, but now what should be a minor problem at one vendor for their customers turns into a major problem because so many people are affected by it.

## Movies

### The Mesa of Lost Women

**Short plot summary**: There's some spider people, but they're just people really, and some other people that die in the desert.

It's rare I watch a movie that makes me think "this is about as bad as Red Zone Cuba."  **The Mesa of Lost Women** is a film which is thoroughly incompetent at every level. There's no real plot driving it forward, what happens before the plane crash mostly seems unrelated to what happens after the crash. We have an overbearing narrator that keeps talking, and then as soon as he does I wanted him back because the flamenco guitar soundtrack got old before the dance sequence ended.

It's like if you took Plan 9 and stripped out its earnest attempt at being an interesting and entertaining film, and instead stretched a 10 minute short to feature length.

Reading about the production / making off on Wikipedia is better than actually watching this movie.

I can't even tell you the plot because it's so disjointed that it doesn't even matter.

## Anime

### Nadia: The Secret of Blue Water

**Short plot summary**: Circus performer with a priceless jewel somehow escapes France with a teenage boy named Jean, goes on an adventure to Atlantis, and then averts the end of the world.

This show was pretty wild. Last week I think I was around episode 15 to 20 or so, and things just got more wild from there. On one side you have our protagonists, who are doing a romance play, on the other side you've got two factions of Atlanteans warring together for the fate of the world.

The Atlantis stuff was compelling.

The teen romance was compelling.

Except... someone needs to calm Nadia down. I don't know why Jean took that abuse, she's like a proto-Asuka except somehow flies off the handle even more quickly over even more trivial things. SHe takes every opportunity to remind Jean that she's a vegetarian (not a vegan, she eats eggs but not fish), and considers self defense to be the same thing as blowing up the city of Paris with an alien space laser.

Jean is very clearly intoxicated when she takes advantage at him, but she's the one that gets mad when he can't remember what she did to him without his consent.

The side characters also get a lot of love, and feel like fully developed characters. Again, this is heavily a mark of Evangelion where some of the most compelling material is from the side characters.

Heck in the final sequence, Elektra comes out wearing what is essentially a proto-plugsuit design.

The last few episodes might as well have been part of Neon Genesis Evangelion. You can see that many of the same people worked on the two shows, there's stuff in the imagery copied from Nadia to Eva, and the framing and such in the final confrontation of Nadia are literally speaking the same language as Evangelion did for most of its run. There's even parallels in the characters, Nadia is very much like Asuka, Shinji is like Jean if you stripped all his motivation, intelligence, and skill away. ...Wow I really dissed Shinji there, but everyone's lives would have been better if he'd just got in the dang robot.

Overall Nadia was a pretty good show. I wish I'd watched it years ago instead of catching it in re-re-re-re-runs on RetroCrunch's linear channel. I can recommend it if you want a fun and interesting adventure set near the turn of the 19th to 20th centuries.

### Sol Bianca

**Short plot summary**: Some lady space pirates kidnap a kid, then go to steal some treasure and end up fighting a planet named "Tres" that is oppressing "Uno."

YouTube bubbled this up as a recommendation for me and I gave it a go. The main thing I've taken away from it is that it was mostly nice to look at. The early 90's animation is still nice to look at, and the pirates had some nice interactions with each other, but overall it just didn't click for me, either in the combat sequences or the story. Somehow the evil bad guy has a treasure trove, including what appears to be a better version of the golden record from Voyager I and people who are like frozen in carbonite or something.

A lot of people would take this as a stronger statement than I intend it: It was okay.

Apparently the English dub that I watched only covered the first episode of the OVA and the second half was untranslated and undubbed into English. Not sure what the story is behind that, but given the kind of things that ADV and other companies started dubbing by the mid-2000s in the anime DVD boom it seems weird the other episode never got translated.

### Cyber City Oedo 808

**Short plot summary**: Cyber criminals hired by cybercops to catch cybercriminals means that they're fighting the military and hunting vampires.

Cyber City Oedo 808 is a three episode OVA set in the year 2808 in the city of Oedo which I'm pretty sure is renamed Tokyo. Each episode centers around a member of a trio of cyber criminals let loose by the government to fight other cyber crooks. In one, there's people trapped in a building, in another they tangle with a secret army project, and the third is vampire-related.

The show is really nice looking, and you can see the director's (Yoshiaki Kawajiri) style in literally every frame. While this was nice enough to watch, and a lot of fun (and solidly a 7/10 for good action), it really made me want to go watch Vampire Hunter D: Bloodlust which is apparently not available for streaming anywhere.

### Appleseed (1988)

**Short plot summary**: A woman unalives herself (to use the modern vernacular) which leads to society nearly collapsing. And there are cyborg cops.

I remember watching this probably around 25 years ago and not being terribly impressed with it. I've never read Appleseed the manga, but I've both read other Shirow works and seen the adaptations of them. This didn't seem to have the chaotic charm of Dominion or Ghost in the Shell. Watching it again now, recorded off RetroCrush's FAST channel, I've certainly enjoyed it more.

The motivations of the characters and the plot makes sense, the action is good, and I found the old dub fun. I swear I recognize most of these VAs from other anime dubbed in this time period. And the cyberpunk Utopia of Olympus is a neat setting that I wish I could experience more of, just like the dystopia in Tank Police.

I think I gotta give it something like a 7/10 because I sure enjoyed it and it sure didn't waste any time.

Maybe the funniest part is I'm pretty sure my DVD of it is in the cabinet right under the TV, but here I am watching it DVR'd off TV instead. I don't know what that means.
