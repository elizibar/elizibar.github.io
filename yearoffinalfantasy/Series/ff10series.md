# Final Fantasy X Series

*Final Fantasy X* (2001) is the tenth mainline entry in the series and the first to be released on the PlayStation 2. It follows Tidus, a blitzball star from Zanarkand, who is pulled into the world of Spira and joins the summoner Yuna on her pilgrimage to defeat the monster known as Sin. The game is known for its emotional story, the relationship between Tidus and Yuna, the sphere grid character progression system, and the underwater sport of blitzball.

It is also the first mainline Final Fantasy to receive a direct numbered sequel.

### Final Fantasy X-2

*Final Fantasy X-2* (2003) is set two years after the events of Final Fantasy X. It follows Yuna, Rikku, and a new character named Paine as they travel Spira as sphere hunters. The tone is noticeably lighter and more playful than its predecessor, with a dressphere job system that lets the characters switch combat roles on the fly.

Reception for X-2 has always been more mixed than for X. Some players enjoy the faster combat, the job system, and the chance to spend more time with the characters. Others find the shift in tone jarring and the story less compelling. It remains one of the more debated direct sequels in the series.

### Ports and the HD Remaster

Both games received international versions with additional content. In 2013–2014 they were brought together as *Final Fantasy X | X-2 HD Remaster* for PlayStation 3 and Vita, later released on PlayStation 4, PC, Nintendo Switch, Xbox One, and more recently Nintendo Switch 2. The HD Remaster includes remastered visuals, rearranged and original soundtrack options, and various quality-of-life features (especially on later platforms).

### Other Notes

Characters from Final Fantasy X have appeared in crossover titles such as the *Dissidia Final Fantasy* series. The world of Spira has otherwise remained relatively self-contained compared to the much larger expanded universes of VII or XIII.

Reception for Final Fantasy X itself is generally very strong. It is frequently cited as one of the high points of the series for its story, characters, music, and presentation. The HD Remaster is widely regarded as the definitive way to play both games on modern hardware.
