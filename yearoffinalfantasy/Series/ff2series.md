# Final Fantasy II Series

*Final Fantasy II* (1988) is the second mainline entry in the series. It was developed by Square for the Famicom and originally released only in Japan. The game follows Firion and his allies as they join a rebellion against the expanding Palamecian Empire. It is notable for introducing a skill-based progression system where characters improve the abilities they use rather than gaining traditional experience levels, a design that remains divisive among players.

A quick note on the naming: when Square brought the series to North America, they skipped the real Final Fantasy II and III. The game released in the US as "Final Fantasy II" on the Super Nintendo was actually *Final Fantasy IV*. The true Final Fantasy II did not see an official English release until the PlayStation *Origins* collection in 2003.

Major versions and ports include:

- **Famicom** (December 17, 1988, Japan) - The original release.
- **WonderSwan Color** (2001, Japan) - First major visual upgrade.
- **PlayStation - Final Fantasy Origins** (2002-2003) - Enhanced version that brought the game to Western audiences for the first time.
- **Game Boy Advance - Final Fantasy I & II: Dawn of Souls** (2004) - Added quality-of-life improvements and bonus content.
- **PlayStation Portable - 20th Anniversary Edition** (2007) - Further refinements and additional features.
- **Mobile / iOS / Android** (various, 2010 onward)
- **Pixel Remaster** (2021 on Steam/mobile; 2023 on Switch/PS4; 2024 on Xbox) - Modern pixel-art remaster with rearranged soundtrack and quality-of-life options.

Reception has long been mixed. The story and characters are often praised relative to the first game, while the unique leveling system is frequently cited as a source of frustration due to the grinding it can encourage. Later ports and the Pixel Remaster have made the game more approachable for modern players.
