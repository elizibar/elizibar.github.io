# Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles Series

*Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles* is a spin-off series that began with the 2003 GameCube title of the same name. It was developed by The Game Designers Studio (a Square Enix subsidiary) and is set in a shared world populated by four tribes – Clavats, Lilties, Yukes, and Selkies – that must contend with a poisonous miasma and the crystals that protect them from it. The series is known for experimental multiplayer ideas, a more fairy-tale tone than many mainline entries, and a strong emphasis on cooperative play in its earlier titles.

### Main Games

- **Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles** (2003, GameCube) – The original. A caravan of villagers travels the world collecting myrrh to keep their crystal alive. Notable for requiring Game Boy Advance systems as controllers for multiplayer.
- **Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: Ring of Fates** (2007, Nintendo DS) – A prequel focused on twin siblings Yuri and Chelinka.
- **Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: Echoes of Time** (2009, Nintendo DS / Wii) – A sequel to Ring of Fates that supported cross-play between the two systems.
- **Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: The Crystal Bearers** (2009, Wii) – A later single-player action title set long after the original, with a more open structure and different tone.

Two WiiWare titles also belong to the series:

- **My Life as a King** (2008) – A city-building / kingdom-management game set after the original.
- **My Life as a Darklord** (2009) – A tower-defense style sequel focused on the Darklord’s daughter.

Both WiiWare games have long since been delisted.

### Remaster

**Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: Remastered Edition** (2020) brought the original GameCube game to modern platforms (Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 4, iOS, Android) with online multiplayer, new content, and updated presentation.

### Other Media

A three-volume manga adaptation of the original game, *Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: Beyond the Endless Sky* (art by Ryunosuke Ichikawa), was released in Japan. A short 4-panel anthology manga for Ring of Fates also exists. Neither received an official English release.

### Reception and Place in the Franchise

The series has always occupied a slightly separate corner of Final Fantasy. The original GameCube game is remembered for its ambitious (if cumbersome) multiplayer design and distinctive soundtrack. Later entries experimented with different genres and met with mixed results. The 2020 remaster helped keep the first game accessible, but most of the sequels remain harder to play legally today.
