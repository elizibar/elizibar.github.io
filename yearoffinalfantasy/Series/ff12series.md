# Final Fantasy XII Series

*Final Fantasy XII* (2006) is the twelfth mainline entry in the series. It was developed by Square Enix for the PlayStation 2 and is set in the world of Ivalice. The game follows Vaan, Ashe, Basch, Balthier, Fran, and Penelo as they become entangled in the political and military struggles between the empires of Archadia and Rozarria, as well as the deeper history of the Occuria and the nethicite. It is known for its open exploration, the gambit system for party AI, and a more political, less purely personal story than many earlier entries.

### Ivalice Alliance

Final Fantasy XII is part of the broader *Ivalice Alliance*, a collection of games set in the shared world of Ivalice. Other titles in the Alliance include *Final Fantasy Tactics: The War of the Lions*, *Final Fantasy Tactics A2: Grimoire of the Rift*, and *Final Fantasy XII: Revenant Wings*. While the games are not always direct sequels to one another, they share setting, races, and thematic elements.

### Final Fantasy XII: Revenant Wings

*Final Fantasy XII: Revenant Wings* (2007, Nintendo DS) is a real-time strategy RPG sequel set one year after the events of Final Fantasy XII. It follows Vaan and Penelo as sky pirates and introduces the floating continent of Lemurés along with the aegyl race. The tone is lighter than the original game, and the gameplay shifts to a more tactical, real-time style with controllable summons. It was the first title explicitly announced as part of the Ivalice Alliance.

### International Zodiac Job System and The Zodiac Age

In 2007 Square Enix released *Final Fantasy XII International Zodiac Job System* in Japan. This version replaced the original single license board with twelve job-specific boards, added a speed-up option, new equipment, and other adjustments. It significantly changed how players approach character builds and party composition.

*Final Fantasy XII: The Zodiac Age* (2017) is a high-definition remaster based on the International Zodiac Job System version. It brought the job system and other improvements to Western audiences for the first time, along with updated visuals, a remastered soundtrack, and quality-of-life features. It is widely regarded as the definitive way to play the game.

### Reception

Reception for the original Final Fantasy XII was strong but somewhat divided. Many praised the world, music, and systems, while others found the main characters less compelling or the gambit system too automated. The Zodiac Age version has been received more consistently positively, with the job system and quality-of-life improvements addressing several common complaints about the original release.
