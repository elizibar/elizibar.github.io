# Final Fantasy XIII Series

*Final Fantasy XIII* (2009) is the thirteenth mainline entry in the series. It was developed by Square Enix primarily for PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360. The game follows Lightning and a group of companions who are turned into l'Cie and forced to fulfill a Focus given by the fal'Cie, all while navigating the conflict between the floating world of Cocoon and the lower world of Pulse. It is known for its linear structure in the early and mid-game, a fast-paced paradigm-shifting battle system, and a heavy emphasis on cinematic presentation.

It became the foundation of a larger project called *Fabula Nova Crystallis Final Fantasy*, which was originally intended to include multiple games sharing mythology and themes. In practice, the main story continued directly through two sequels focused on Lightning and her circle.

### Final Fantasy XIII-2

*Final Fantasy XIII-2* (2011) is set a few years after the end of the first game. Lightning has disappeared, and her sister Serah teams up with a mysterious young man named Noel to travel through time and resolve paradoxes that threaten the future. The game opens up the structure considerably compared to XIII, adds a monster-collecting system, and continues the mythological threads involving the goddess Etro and the warrior Caius Ballad.

Reception was mixed-to-positive. Many players appreciated the greater freedom and the time-travel premise, while others found the story more convoluted and the ending polarizing.

### Lightning Returns: Final Fantasy XIII

*Lightning Returns: Final Fantasy XIII* (2013) serves as the conclusion to the trilogy. Set 500 years after XIII-2, the world is dying and Lightning is tasked with saving the souls of humanity before the end. The gameplay shifts to a single-character action-oriented style with a strong emphasis on time management, equipment, and schedule-based quests across a fixed number of in-game days.

Reception was again mixed. Some players liked the more action-focused combat and the sense of finality, while others found the structure and tone uneven. Together the three games form a complete (if divisive) arc.

### Fabula Nova Crystallis and Related Titles

The broader *Fabula Nova Crystallis* project also included *Final Fantasy Type-0* (originally *Agito XIII*) and what eventually became *Final Fantasy XV* (originally *Versus XIII*). Those games share some mythological DNA but are not direct continuations of the Lightning trilogy and are usually treated as separate entries.

### Ports and Availability

The three main games have been released on multiple platforms over the years, including PC. They are often sold together in trilogy bundles.

Reception for the series as a whole remains split. Final Fantasy XIII itself is frequently criticized for its linearity and story presentation, yet it also has a dedicated following that values its combat, production values, and characters. The sequels attempted to address some of the common complaints while introducing new ones. It stands as one of the more debated corners of the modern Final Fantasy lineup.
