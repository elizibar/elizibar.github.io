# Final Fantasy XI Series

*Final Fantasy XI* (2002) is the eleventh mainline entry in the series and the first massively multiplayer online game (MMO) in the Final Fantasy lineup. Originally released on PlayStation 2 and PC (with an Xbox 360 version following later), it is set in the world of Vana'diel. Players create their own adventurer and join others in a persistent online world filled with jobs, crafting, exploration, and large-scale multiplayer content.

It is also the first Final Fantasy title to receive traditional expansion packs, a model that would later influence how Square Enix approached long-running online games.

### Expansions and Major Updates

Final Fantasy XI received five major expansion packs:

- **Rise of the Zilart** (2003) - The first expansion. Added new areas, story, and raised the level cap.
- **Chains of Promathia** (2004) - Continued the main story with a darker tone and new regions.
- **Treasures of Aht Urhgan** (2006) - Introduced the Near East, new jobs, and large-scale conflict content.
- **Wings of the Goddess** (2007) - Added a time-travel storyline set during the Crystal War, along with new areas and systems.
- **Seekers of Adoulin** (2013) - The final major retail expansion, focused on the continent of Ulbuka and colonization efforts.

In addition to the expansions, the game has received numerous free add-on scenarios, story updates, quality-of-life improvements, and seasonal events over the years. Service has continued for more than two decades, with the game still receiving periodic updates.

### Platforms and Current Status

Final Fantasy XI launched on PlayStation 2 and Windows PC. An Xbox 360 version was released later. Official support for the console versions eventually ended, leaving PC as the primary platform. The game remains active, with both official servers and a notable private-server community.

### Reception and Legacy

Reception has always been mixed in the broader gaming press due to the game’s age, steep learning curve, and older design conventions (such as heavy reliance on parties for much of the experience). Among players who stuck with it, however, it is often praised for its world-building, sense of community, job system, and the depth of its long-running story. It holds an important place in the series as Square’s first major experiment with the MMO format and as a direct predecessor in spirit to *Final Fantasy XIV*.
