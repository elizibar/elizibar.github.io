# Final Fantasy Series

*Final Fantasy* (1987) is the game that launched the entire franchise. Developed by Square for the Famicom (NES in the West), it introduced many of the series' foundational elements: the Warriors of Light, the four elemental crystals and Fiends, class-based party creation, turn-based combat, and Nobuo Uematsu's iconic score. It was a commercial and critical success that helped popularize console RPGs outside Japan and saved Square from near-bankruptcy at the time.

The original game has been ported, remade, and re-released more times than almost any other entry in the series. Major versions include:

- **Famicom / NES** (1987 Japan / 1990 North America) - The original. Straightforward but notoriously grindy and full of quirks/bugs. Still the purest "as designed" experience for many purists.
- **MSX2** (1989, Japan only) - Minor graphical and audio upgrades over the Famicom version.
- **WonderSwan Color** (2000, Japan) - First major visual overhaul with redrawn sprites and battle backgrounds. Formed the basis for later console ports.
- **PlayStation - Final Fantasy Origins** (2002-2003) - Based on the WonderSwan version. Added FMVs, a bestiary, music player, and improved presentation. First official Western release of the enhanced version.
- **Game Boy Advance - Final Fantasy I & II: Dawn of Souls** (2004) - Significant quality-of-life changes, four brand-new bonus dungeons, a bestiary, and rebalanced difficulty. Widely regarded as one of the best classic versions for years.
- **PlayStation Portable - 20th Anniversary Edition** (2007) - Further refinements of the GBA version, additional content, and higher resolution.
- **Mobile / iOS / Android / Windows Phone** (various, starting 2004-2012) - Several iterations, some based on earlier ports.
- **Pixel Remaster** (2021 on Steam/mobile; 2023 on Switch/PS4; 2024 on Xbox) - Modern pixel-art remaster with rearranged orchestral-style soundtrack, quality-of-life features (auto-battle, dash, etc.), and a return to the original spell-charge magic system. Generally liked for its presentation and accessibility, though some players miss the extra dungeons from the GBA/PSP versions.

Reception has been consistently positive across eras. The original was praised for its graphics, music, and ambition at the time, while later ports are usually judged on how well they modernize the experience without losing the classic feel. The Pixel Remaster is currently the most accessible and polished way for most new players to experience the game.

### Stranger of Paradise: Final Fantasy Origin

Released in 2022, *Stranger of Paradise: Final Fantasy Origin* is an action-RPG spin-off developed by Team Ninja (of *Nioh* and *Ninja Gaiden* fame) and published by Square Enix. It is set in a darker, alternate-universe take on the world of the original *Final Fantasy*, following Jack Garland and his companions as they set out to "kill Chaos."

Rather than a direct prequel or remake, it reimagines key locations, characters, and themes from the 1987 game through fast, high-difficulty action combat and a job system. The story starts straightforward and gradually reveals a much stranger (and more metafictional) connection to the original *Final Fantasy*. Reception was mixed: critics and players generally liked the combat and sense of style, while noting that the narrative and characters take time to fully click.
