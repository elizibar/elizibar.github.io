# Final Fantasy VI Series

*Final Fantasy VI* (1994) is the sixth mainline entry in the series. It was developed by Square for the Super Famicom and is often regarded as one of the high points of the 2D era. The game features a large ensemble cast, a story that shifts from rebellion against an empire to a more personal struggle against a world-ending threat, and the magicite system that lets characters learn spells and abilities from Espers. It is frequently praised for its characters, music, and the scale of its narrative.

A note on the naming: when Square localized the series for North America, they skipped the real Final Fantasy II and III. The game released in the US as "Final Fantasy III" on the Super Nintendo was actually *Final Fantasy VI*. Later re-releases restored the correct numbering.

The original North American SNES version also saw a number of content changes to meet Nintendo of America’s guidelines at the time. Religious references were softened (Holy became Pearl, for example), some dialogue was cleaned up, and several sprites were retouched. Female Espers and certain enemy sprites had clothing extended or adjusted to reduce exposure, cigarette smoke was removed from some character sprites, and pub signs were changed to cafes. Later ports and the Pixel Remaster restored much of the original presentation and used more accurate translations.

Major versions and ports include:

- **Super Famicom / SNES** (April 2, 1994 Japan / October 11, 1994 North America) - The original release. The North American version carried the Final Fantasy III title and the censorship changes noted above.
- **PlayStation** (1999) - First time the game was released outside Japan under its correct number. Included in collections with added FMV sequences.
- **Game Boy Advance - Final Fantasy VI Advance** (2006-2007) - Added new Espers, a bonus dungeon, and quality-of-life features.
- **Mobile / iOS / Android / PC** (2014-2015)
- **Pixel Remaster** (February 23, 2022 on Steam/mobile; 2023 on Switch/PS4; 2024 on Xbox) - Modern 2D remaster with rearranged soundtrack, updated pixel art, and modern quality-of-life options. Uses a cleaner translation and largely uncensored visuals.

Reception has been consistently excellent. Final Fantasy VI is widely considered one of the strongest entries in the series for its story, cast, and soundtrack. The Pixel Remaster is generally viewed as a clean and accessible way to experience the game, though some players still prefer earlier versions for specific bonus content or presentation choices.
