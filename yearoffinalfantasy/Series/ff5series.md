# Final Fantasy V Series

*Final Fantasy V* (1992) is the fifth mainline entry in the series. It was developed by Square for the Super Famicom and is best known for its deep and flexible job system, which lets characters freely change classes and mix abilities. The story follows Bartz, Lenna, Galuf, Faris, and later Krile as they try to protect the crystals from the threat of Exdeath. Compared to its neighbors in the series, it has a lighter, more adventurous tone with a strong focus on gameplay experimentation.

Major versions and ports include:

- **Super Famicom** (December 6, 1992, Japan) - The original release. It did not receive an official Western release at the time.
- **PlayStation - Final Fantasy Anthology** (1998-1999) - First official English release, bundled with Final Fantasy VI in the West.
- **Game Boy Advance - Final Fantasy V Advance** (2006) - Added new jobs, a bonus dungeon, and quality-of-life improvements.
- **Mobile / iOS / Android / PC** (2013-2015)
- **Pixel Remaster** (November 10, 2021 on Steam/mobile; 2023 on Switch/PS4; 2024 on Xbox) - Modern 2D remaster with rearranged soundtrack and updated presentation.

Reception has generally been positive, especially among players who enjoy systems-heavy RPGs. The job system is frequently cited as one of the most influential and replayable in the series, even if the story is often considered lighter than Final Fantasy IV or VI.

### Final Fantasy: Legend of the Crystals

*Final Fantasy: Legend of the Crystals* is a four-episode OVA released in 1994. It is set roughly 200 years after the events of Final Fantasy V and follows a new cast dealing with a fresh threat to the crystals. It holds the distinction of being one of the earliest official sequels to a mainline Final Fantasy game.

Reception has been mixed. Some contemporary reviews were positive, but retrospective views tend to be cooler. Critics have noted that the animation is serviceable for its time, yet the story feels thin, the tone leans heavily into comedy, and it does not capture much of the spirit of the original game. It remains a curiosity rather than a highly regarded part of the Final Fantasy V legacy.
