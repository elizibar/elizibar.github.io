# Final Fantasy XVI

**System**: Steam (PC)  
**Release Date**: September 17, 2024  
**Related Sub-Series**: Final Fantasy XVI  
**Date Played**: February 2, 2025 through July 20, 2025  

### Verdict

I generally enjoyed Final Fantasy XVI. It is a gorgeous game on PC, though the performance requirements were brutal - I had to close the Steam UI after launching just to squeeze out every last bit of smoothness. Combat felt fast, fluid, and satisfying most of the time, but some sequences (especially certain boss fights) went on far too long, and the constant attention demanded by the pure action gameplay could get exhausting.

Going in, the only real expectation I had was the "it's like Game of Thrones" talk floating around on Twitter/X. That comparison was wrong. What I got instead was a solid, spectacle-heavy action game with a surprisingly deep gnostic streak in its plot. Making a mainline Final Fantasy a pure action title was a bold choice. Some of the set-piece fights were enormous and memorable - the Titan sequence in particular felt endless while I was playing it, yet pieces of it are still stuck in my head. Seeing the world visibly fall apart around the characters, and watching the consequences land on ordinary people, gave the story real weight.

Benedikta stood out as a strong character with clear motivations and a solid backstory. The wider cast (Cid, Clive, Hugo, and others) carried a lot of personality. The setting itself is one of the most tragic fictional worlds I can think of - magic is rare and its users are oppressed, the land is decaying, and the creator figure (whether you call him pantokrator or demiurge) fundamentally hates his own creation. The story kept surprising me; I rarely knew where it was heading.

I hated the ending for two reasons. First, it is a deeply unhappy ending for Clive and I wanted better for him. Second, the final moments float the possibility that the events might only be an in-universe fiction, which undercuts the struggles that came before. A lot of the side-quests were also pure filler with almost no personality, which stood out badly in a game that otherwise has so much character.

The Leviathan fight in the DLC felt noticeably more unforgiving than the rest of the game - a pure DPS check that demanded near-optimal play instead of letting you approach it your own way. Most other bosses (including Omega and the main-game encounters) were fun, cool, and fair once you learned them. Regular new abilities and powers helped keep combat from feeling samey. Having to buy DLC just to experience the "complete" game still felt strange for a mainline Final Fantasy, even knowing XV, XIV, and XI had their own extra content.

Some of the soundtrack tracks were excellent. The menus took a little getting used to, but once they clicked the whole package felt cohesive. A few of the achievements look overly grindy (mastering every ability, etc.), so I'm not looking forward to those. I do plan to go back and try some of the Action replays of stages to see how that changes the experience.

It is hard to compare Final Fantasy XVI to any other game in the series. Right now it just sits as its own thing. If you like sword-slashing action games and have the hardware to run it well, it is an easy recommendation.
