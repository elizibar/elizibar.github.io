# Final Fantasy Tactics: The Ivalice Chronicles

**System**: Steam (PC)  
**Release Date**: September 30, 2025  
**Related Sub-Series**: [Final Fantasy Tactics Series](/yearoffinalfantasy/fftseries/), [Ivalice Alliance](/yearoffinalfantasy/ivalice/)  
**Date Played**: October 5, 2026 through October 20, 2026  

### Verdict

Final Fantasy Tactics is a great game, and The Ivalice Chronicles feels mostly like the definitive version. This release folds in War of the Lions cutscenes from the PSP version, restores Sound Novel content that was cut from the original US PSX release, and still left room to dive straight into the deep end of modding.

At the time of the original PSX release, at least in the US, a tactical combat RPG was a pretty rare thing. The US did not have Front Mission, and if Tactics Ogre was around I do not remember ever seeing it. This was a fresh style of gameplay paired with a very serious plot that borders on Shakespeare. Expectations going in were high, and they were met.

Riovanes Castle is still the big exclamation point - high point for difficulty, huge narrative moment, and a sequence that is unforgettable. The story is deep, with layers of betrayal like wandering into Hamlet. Somewhere along the way I made a joke image tying Tactics, Hamlet, and the anime Scarlet into a related series called "Denmark." In this version's translation the characters have so much personality that even the minor villains stand out. Voice acting and translation were fantastic; the cast went all out. Hearing Clive's voice actor show up as an evil bastard was a fun touch. Soundtrack is fantastic too - battle music stays engaging, and the rest fits every scene.

The main mechanical pain is experience gain. Leveling low-level characters is rough, and chasing job mastery can wreck stat growth. If you are the kind of person who tries to optimize characters, Tactics will let you optimize the fun right out of the game. That system could have used some love. Difficulty is really only hard early on and at Riovanes. The rest of the game can be rolled over with only modest challenge, which leaves plenty of room to experiment with different party combinations. The optional dungeon is a strange experience that flips a lot of the game's rules - preferring low Bravery to find hidden treasures. Hard to imagine how many hours it would take without a guide.

On PC, being able to mod heavily changed the feel of the whole run. Lavian and Alicia as Blue and Red Mages, a mod that put Gafgarion into the party after his defeat, editing another character into Isilude so he could join - all of that led to some pretty funny moments. Controlling the experience with mods gave the game a new, fun appreciation and breathed real life back into it. I wish modding were easier. The learning curve looks rough, and I still had ideas left on the table.

I would absolutely recommend this version, unless someone is already deep in the Hacktics community, which I think is still mostly built around the PSX release. If the grind were not so bad I would replay it, but as it is I do not really want to grind this hard again. There is a New Game+ mode after the credits, which might take some of the sting out of another run.
