# Final Fantasy V Pixel Remaster

**System**: Steam (PC)  
**Release Date**: November 10, 2021  
**Related Sub-Series**: [Final Fantasy V Series](/yearoffinalfantasy/ff5series/)  
**Date Played**: April 12, 2026 through April 26, 2026  

### Verdict

Going from The After Years to this Pixel Remaster was joyous.

I had forgotten a lot of the details before this replay, but the Battle at the Big Bridge always stuck with me, and doing it again was a great time. I knew I was going to enjoy the game and I did. This is a strong way to play Final Fantasy V; the Pixel Remaster additions make it a smooth experience.

Standout moments include the Big Bridge fight, the big mid-game sacrifice, and returning to the main character’s hometown with "Home, Sweet Home" playing. The job system is fun and interesting, and Blue Magic plus the process of figuring out how to learn it adds a satisfying layer of experimentation. The downside is that there is so much Blue Magic you would never reasonably discover all of it without either hundreds of hours or a guide.

It may be the "least" of the three SNES Final Fantasies, but it is still a great game. Playing through the early entries in order really shows Square inventing and refining the JRPG genre; in terms of mechanical interest and ambition this one is a clear step up from Final Fantasy IV. The soundtrack has some real standouts. The Big Bridge battle theme is fire, and "Home, Sweet Home" carries a hopeful melancholy that lands hard. Difficulty is reasonable, pacing is brisk, and the overall experience feels good.

The story leans hard into character losses and "the feels," so it can get a bit depressing in places, but the ending payoff is uplifting and satisfying. I don't feel an active need to replay it, it runs a bit long when there are so many other good games waiting. The achievements are frustrating, though. There are a lot of one-time visit areas, so missing a single chest or hidden item can spoil the 100% completion goals.
