# Final Fantasy VII Ever Crisis

**System**: Steam (PC)  
**Release Date**: December 6, 2023  
**Related Sub-Series**: [Final Fantasy VII Series](/yearoffinalfantasy/ff7series/)  
**Date Played**: July 7, 2026 through August 16, 2026, plus content updates on August 27, 2026, August 30, 2026, and September 6, 2026.

### Verdict

I enjoyed Ever Crisis. It was neat being able to play it on my phone as well as a computer, and it has great art direction. On the positive side it was pretty and did a good job of delivering the cliff notes versions of Final Fantasy VII storylines. On the negative side, when someone invented the phrase "dark patterns" they were talking about this kind of design. Everything about it is meant to compulsively drive engagement toward spending money.

I'd never played a gacha game before, so I wasn't sure what to expect going in. I got a somewhat simplistic combat engine and a quite deep character-building system with dozens of interacting pathways toward synergizing the character. The game is being shut down, so a recommendation around playing it seems moot. It was a nice way to sample FF7 stories I'd yet to experience, like Crisis Core.

One thing that stood out as real strange was the Advent Children section. We basically just watched Advent Children in the game, interrupted with a short combat segment. Most of the rest of the game had in-engine cutscenes, so that part felt different.

The writing in the First Soldier chapters was heinous. Those characters did not start out likable and didn't get likable until near the end of the first half of the First Soldier story. I really liked how each system I engaged with - Events, Guilds, the Dungeons - helped power up characters in different ways. The First Soldier chapters seemed to be new content, and they did feel subpar compared to the other stories.

It was more fun than Dirge of Cerberus, but not as fun as Final Fantasy VII itself. For me it was a novel experience, and since I don't know that I'll play another gacha game, it's going to remain rather unique.

The soundtrack borrowed its themes from FF7 and had some killer remixes of those tunes. Art design was superb. I liked all the different outfits you could pick for characters, like a Marching Band style look for Cait Sith or Tifa wearing clothes modeled after her martial arts mentor.

I played after the big end-of-life party started, so the game just gave me tons of resources to push on through. Most of it was pretty easy with some grinding of various resources, but a few fights like B100 of the battle tower presented challenges that I had to step back and plan out instead of just taking the auto-equip's choices.

It tells the story of most Final Fantasy VII related media prior to the Remake project - the original game, Crisis Core, Dirge of Cerberus, and more. No, I would never start over in a gacha style game. That's like deciding to work an extra job, unpaid, all over again instead of playing a game for fun.

The achievements seemed reasonable. The grinding was always toward a purpose, and it was always within reach, and on a day to day basis it didn't take up too much time. The achievements were like being incentivized to visit an art museum daily.
