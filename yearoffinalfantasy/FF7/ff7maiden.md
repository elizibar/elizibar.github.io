# Hoshi o Meguru Otome (The Maiden Who Travels The Planet)

**System**: Book  
**Release Date**: September 9, 2005  
**Related Sub-Series**: [Final Fantasy VII Series](/yearoffinalfantasy/ff7series/)  
**Date Played**: October 5, 2026  

### Verdict

Overall it was okay. A little clunky to read, which might just be the unofficial translation. I read the one available at LHYeung.net and it could have used an editor. There are other fan translations out there, but I really wish there had been an official one and that it had been included as preface material in *On the Way to a Smile*.

I liked how the Lifestream is depicted - familiar but otherworldly. It felt appropriate. The pacing is good too; it doesn't linger anywhere for too long and keeps moving as Aerith goes on a kind of Dante-style journey through different areas and people.

Three moments stood out: Aerith meeting the AVALANCHE group (Jessie, Biggs, and Wedge) and talking through their feelings after the Sector 1 bombing; the moment she realizes Sephiroth is in the Lifestream too while she's trying to leave the Shinra president behind; and her meeting with Zack. That last one felt the least canonical of everything in the story - something about the interaction felt a little off - but I loved her initial reaction to seeing him and realizing he hadn't just moved on with life, he was dead too.

Kind of a greatest-hits tour of FF7 characters. We get Jessie and crew, the Shinra president, Dyne, Zack, even Hojo. Seeing the events of FF7 from Aerith's perspective in the Lifestream was interesting, and her desperation to help even when she couldn't was touching.

I didn't actually like the very start, where Aerith is conscious and falling into the water / falling into death. That felt odd and I'm not quite sure what the author was going for, unless it was meant to make the Lifestream feel more like a near-death or out-of-body experience, at least for a Cetra.

Compared to the other Final Fantasy VII books this novella wasn't that fun to read. It was alright. Reading the bare text in a PDF wasn't a great experience either. I have no desire to ever reread it.

I want to take a moment to thank Benny Matsuyama for writing the story, and the translator for providing a version of what would otherwise be completely inaccessible.
