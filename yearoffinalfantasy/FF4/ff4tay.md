# Final Fantasy IV: The After Years

**System**: Steam (PC)  
**Release Date**: May 11, 2015  
**Related Sub-Series**: [Final Fantasy IV Series](/yearoffinalfantasy/ff4series/)  
**Date Played**: March 10, 2026 through April 10, 2026  

### Verdict

Disappointed.

The biggest standout is how poor the writing is across almost every scenario. One section has you hunting down a coconut so you can make fuel for a boat. I do not even know how a plot reaches that point. I went in with modest expectations and for the most part the game failed to meet them. I would not recommend it to anyone.

I lost track of how many times the game revisits the same areas across different scenarios, especially the waterfall cave. Asset reuse is fine in principle, but this really pushes it. A lot of the writing is intentionally vague or simply refuses to show the player what is actually happening, and when characters do talk they tend to be wordy without saying much. Ceodore’s name alone (Cecil’s kid) immediately undercut any suspension of disbelief right at the start.

Coming straight off the Final Fantasy IV Pixel Remaster, the 3D presentation here felt clunky instead of charming. I believe most of the music is reused from IV. Difficulty is uneven; some chapters (Golbez’s, or the solo Edward stretch) get genuinely rough. The story itself is a dim echo of Final Fantasy IV’s plot, complete with another off-world threat, told in fits and starts full of sound and fury that ultimately signify very little.

When I first heard about an episodic sequel I assumed the chapters would be short, something you could finish in about 30 minutes on a commute. Instead each installment runs 2–4 hours. Characters constantly act shocked by events in ways that contradict how they were written in the original game. It almost feels like a parody of IV, except it is not funny. A couple of chapters did land better for me (the ninja-focused one, and some of the final dungeon material), but they were the exception.

The new moon-phase mechanic does not add much. The group attacks are a natural extension of Twin magic from Palom and Porom and are neat in concept, but unlocking them for completion is yet another grind. Optional rare drops in every chapter take the already disrespectful rare-encounter design from IV and turn it into a new art form of wasting the player’s time.

I have no desire to ever see this game again or think about it.
