# F.E.A.R.

**System**: PC  
**Release Date**: October 18, 2005  
**Played During**: FEARtober 2017, FEARtober 2025 (Prototype Mod), FEARtober 2026 (EchoPatch)

### Verdict

I'm not sure exactly when it was that I first played F.E.A.R. It had to be sometime not too long after it came out, maybe in 2006 or 2007. That was an age ago, while I was working on my PhD thesis. The shooting gameplay reminded me of SHOGO: Mobile Armored Division, a great first-person shooter with some rough edges that had you run around both on foot and in a giant robot. It was a unique premise at the time, though not so unique now that Titanfall exists. This shouldn't be a surprise, since the same development team worked on both games.

While SHOGO leaned heavy into its anime aesthetic, F.E.A.R. went a different direction, drawing inspiration from that era of Japanese horror films. Alma very directly takes inspiration from Sadako (Ringu) with other films like Ju-On: The Grudge, Dark Water, and Kairo/Pulse filling in mood and jump scare takes.

These was nothing quite like F.E.A.R. before it. Sure, Doom or Quake could cause a jump scare, and Half-Life had really leaned into environment story-telling, but none of them could match the creepy malevonce of F.E.A.R. combined with frantic action.

It was the scariest game I'd ever played at the same, and the creepiest. The stuff that happens to Alma leading up to the events of the game are beyond horrible, and as the Point Man unraveling the mystery one layer at a time, things only seem to grow ever-worse as you try to save the world from a very personal horror.

The only part of the game that can be described as weak is the final segment, fighting ghostly apparitions that are leaking into the real world. It's just not compelling, but everything up to it is solid.

The enemies are pretty smart, and know how to flank the player. They make some use of teamwork, in a way that Half-Life started, but here it was perfected. You're never quite sure what they're going to do. Which is shocking in a world where enemies tended to dumbly rush at the player with no subtelty.

Each part of the game can be defined either as a Story segment, where you get glimpses of Alma or other supernatural events, or as an Action segment, filled with some of the most satisfying shooting action ever put into a game. The weapons are fun, and maybe a second weak point of the game is that by default your arsenal is limited, so you may not get to use the fun weapons like the Nail Gun or the Railgun as much as you might have otherwise enjoyed. Even then, doing something like taking the basic pistols, hitting slo-mo, and pretending you're in a John Woo film feels real good. The only thing missing are some pigeons.

Any fan of first-person shooters or action games should play F.E.A.R.

Though there are some setup issues I forget about until the game has trouble starting, a trip to the Steam forums or a web search solves that. It has a modest modding scene as well, as we've learned in 2025 and 2026's FEARtober event, with the EchoPatch including the fixes. I highly recommend using it to play the game if for nothing else than the quality of life features - plus it and the Prototype Mod both let you increase the number of carried weapons so you can bring fun toys to fights that you wouldn't normally have them available for.

10/10. Legendary. Generational game.
